Our Background

Why IDEA-South Sudan Exists

Founded in 2018 and registered under the NGO Act 2016 with the Ministry of Humanitarian Affairs, Relief and Rehabilitation Commission, IDEA-SS works in partnership with communities, local and national government authorities, UN agencies, international NGOs, and civil society organizations. We design long‑term solutions to alleviate poverty and implement context‑sensitive, rights‑based programmes across South Sudan, promoting social cohesion and empowering communities in tackling the root causes of poverty.

Our approach is grounded in community ownership, gender equality, protection mainstreaming, and accountability to affected populations (AAP). We operate across Jonglei, GPAA, Lake State, Western Equatoria, Eastern Equatoria, Upper Nile, Ruweng Administrative Area, and Central Equatoria.

Who We Are

Mission & Vision

Our Mission

To eradicate poverty in partnership with the communities for sustainable development by improving the living conditions of vulnerable population in hard‑to‑reach communities, socially and economically wellbeing and self‑reliance.

Our Vision

Change the lives of most vulnerable communities through transforming their resourcefulness, made up of skillful development, stability in all areas of lives, and free from poverty, destitution and illiteracy, health, education, peace building, FSL, and gender inequalities issues in South Sudan.

Our Direction

Strategic Objectives

IDEA-SS aims to engage in development programs and empowerment of the most vulnerable communities and marginalized in society to intervene for greater impact on women, men, boys, girls, elderly, and persons with disability.

Promote equitable access to food security and sustainable livelihoods through training and advocacy.

Promote social cohesion, peace building and reconciliation amongst communities in South Sudan.

Provide access to Health, WASH services including potable water, sanitation and hygiene, and Nutrition services to the needy population.

Promote education through training of teachers, advocacy and provision of learning materials in South Sudan.

Empower communities to advance and adopt new skills and knowledge through training to improve their economic capacities.

Provide human rights advocacy and education to communities and civil society stakeholders.

Integrated Pillars

Our Programme Focus

Multi‑sectoral, community‑centered interventions across ten interconnected pillars

WASH

01

Hygiene education, home hygiene promoter training, safe food handling, clean drinking water promotion, and software components for sustainable hygiene practices. We use visual aids to deliver life‑saving messages to communities.

Food Security & Livelihoods

02

Strengthening rural community capacity for crop/livestock production, fishery groups, market linkages, asset creation (dykes, road rehabilitation), and cash‑for‑work. Community‑based financial services and improved food consumption to reduce malnutrition.

Environment & Wildlife

03

Integrated nexus approach focusing on three strategic pillars: Climate Resilience, Ecosystem Restoration, and Community‑Based Wildlife Conservation — recognizing climate change, environmental degradation, and wildlife loss as interconnected threats.

Peacebuilding

04

Building on existing peace structures for conflict resolution and mitigation. Conflict‑sensitive programming, youth peace education, and small income‑generating activities to engage youth in active production and reduce inter‑communal violence.

Disability Inclusion

05

Rehabilitation services, mobility devices, access to education, economic reintegration through business entrepreneurship, psychosocial support, and systematic inclusion of persons with disabilities into livelihoods activities.

Protection & Child Rights

06

SGBV prevention and response, community‑based protection, legal protection, child protection mainstreaming, and protection of persons with specific needs. Participatory approaches to understand root causes of protection violations.

Education

07

Informal and formal education for rural and urban settings. Teacher training, learning materials, village‑level adult education, pastoralist education packages, and equal access campaigns using mass media and community engagement.

Shelter / NFI

08

Life‑saving NFI and emergency shelter materials for conflict‑displaced and returning populations. Vulnerability‑based beneficiary selection prioritizing PWDs, chronically ill, female/child‑headed households, unaccompanied minors, and the elderly. Environmentally conscious distribution.

Health & Nutrition

09

Support to health facilities and feeding centres, routine immunization, maternal child health, and nutrition services in hard‑to‑reach areas with the worst health indicators across South Sudan.

Communication & Advocacy (C4A)

Behavior change communication, social mobilization, engagement of civil society, faith‑based organizations, and private sector. Evidence‑based communication strategies to influence social norms, amplify community voices, and promote human rights approaches.

Our Approach

Methodology & Implementation

A bottom‑up, community‑driven development approach that strengthens individual and community social development

I

Research Stage

Community entry, assessments & surveys, community meetings, asset mapping, problem identification, gap analysis, and collaborative solution design.

II

Design Stage

Writing community‑approved projects, consultation with communities, proposal development, work plans, and budgeting with detailed technical and operational arrangements.

III

Implementation

Exploration, installation, initial and full implementation phases. Carrying out activities described in work plans with active community participation.

IV

Evaluation

Formative and process evaluation during implementation, summative outcome & impact evaluation at completion, and dissemination reporting of significant challenges and successes.

IDEA-SS Community Engagement Actions for Change (CEAC)

Our initiative approach supports communities to engage in modern community‑led activities, combining community‑driven development with humanitarian assistance. Ideas are generated from common people, built upon communities' own resources, solidarity, and skills — using local knowledge and expertise to reduce loss of life and economic impacts from shocks and stresses.
